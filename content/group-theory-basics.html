Group Theory Basics

Group theory is the study of symmetry in mathematics. A group is an algebraic structure that captures the essence of symmetry operations — things you can do, undo, and combine.

Definition of a Group

A group is a set G together with a binary operation · that satisfies four axioms:

  1. Closure: For all a, b in G, the result a · b is also in G.
  2. Associativity: For all a, b, c in G, (a · b) · c = a · (b · c).
  3. Identity: There exists an element e in G such that e · a = a · e = a for all a.
  4. Inverse: For each a in G, there exists a⁻¹ in G such that a · a⁻¹ = a⁻¹ · a = e.

Classic Examples

The Integers under Addition (ℤ, +)

Axiom Check
Closure Sum of two integers is an integer ✓
Associativity (a + b) + c = a + (b + c) ✓
Identity 0 is the identity element ✓
Inverse −a is the inverse of a ✓

Symmetries of a Triangle (D₃)

The dihedral group D₃ consists of 6 symmetries of an equilateral triangle: 3 rotations and 3 reflections. This is a non-abelian group — the order of operations matters.

  • Rotations: 0°, 120°, 240°
  • Reflections: across each of the 3 axes of symmetry

Clock Arithmetic (ℤ/nℤ)

Integers modulo n form a group under addition. For example, ℤ/12ℤ is like a 12-hour clock:

10 + 5 ≡ 3 (mod 12)
 7 + 7 ≡ 2 (mod 12)

Abelian vs Non-Abelian

A group is abelian (or commutative) if a · b = b · a for all elements. Integer addition is abelian. Matrix multiplication, in general, is not.

"The universe is an enormous direct product of representations of symmetry groups." — Steven Weinberg

Subgroups

A subgroup is a subset of a group that is itself a group under the same operation. For example, the even integers form a subgroup of (ℤ, +).

A useful test: H is a subgroup of G if for all a, b in H, the element a · b⁻¹ is also in H.

Why Groups Matter

  • Physics: Symmetry groups describe conservation laws (Noether's theorem).
  • Chemistry: Molecular symmetry groups predict spectroscopic properties.
  • Cryptography: Elliptic curve groups underpin modern public-key encryption.
  • Computer Science: Permutation groups appear in algorithm analysis.

Key Takeaways

  1. A group is a set with an operation satisfying closure, associativity, identity, and inverse.
  2. Groups capture the concept of symmetry abstractly.
  3. Abelian groups have commutative operations; non-abelian ones do not.
  4. Group theory connects algebra, geometry, physics, and computer science.
